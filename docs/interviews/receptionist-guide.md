# Interview guide: clinic receptionist

**Who:** a receptionist, or whoever hands out tokens at a clinic or family doctor's practice. **Time:** 15–20 minutes.
**Goal:** map today's process (the *as-is*), find the pain points, and learn the rules (urgency, bookings, walk-ins) the system must respect.

## Opening (say this)

> "Thank you for your time. I'm building a project about how clinics manage patient queues, for my portfolio. I'm not selling anything and I don't need any patient names or medical details. Is it OK if I take notes? I won't use your name or the clinic's name unless you say I can."

Note: their role, type of clinic (GP, specialist, lab), number of doctors, roughly how many patients a day.

## 1. A normal day (the as-is process)

1. Walk me through what happens from the moment a patient walks in until they see the doctor.
2. How do you give out tokens or numbers today? (paper slips, a register, a board, WhatsApp?)
3. How do patients with an **appointment** fit in with **walk-ins**? Who goes first?
4. What are the busiest times? Roughly how many people are waiting at the peak?
5. How does the doctor tell you they're ready for the next patient?

## 2. Urgent cases and fairness

6. What happens when someone arrives who is clearly more urgent (a child with high fever, chest pain)?
7. Who decides they skip the queue, you or the doctor? Are there any rules?
8. Do other patients complain when someone goes ahead of them? What do you say?
9. Has anyone ever waited a very long time because others kept going ahead? What happened?

## 3. Wait times and information

10. When a patient asks "how long will it take?", what do you tell them? How do you estimate it?
11. Roughly how long does one consultation take? Does it vary a lot?
12. How often do patients leave, or go out and come back? How do you handle someone who misses their turn?

## 4. Problems and tools

13. What is the most frustrating part of managing the queue? Tell me about a recent bad day.
14. Have you ever lost track of who was next, or had an argument about turns? What happened?
15. What tools do you use: computer, phone, register, WhatsApp? Is there reliable internet and a screen in the waiting area?

## 5. Only now: the idea

16. If patients could see their token number and an estimated wait on their phone, and a screen in the waiting room showed "Now serving", what would help? What would worry you?
17. What would make you *not* use a system like this? (time, cost, internet, patients without smartphones…)
18. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?

## Closing

> "Thank you, this really helps. Could I come back to you in a few days to show you what I've built and ask if it makes sense?"

(A yes here is your **pilot clinic** for the PM pilot plan.)
